Executive Brief

Written for ownership. This is the discussion page for our review call

Mark, you've built a real company, and the foundation inside JobTread is stronger than you think. Every active job lives in the system. Your cost catalog is genuinely well-organized, your vendor and sub records are complete, and your team didn't push back on this review. They leaned into it. That matters, because the most expensive part of any implementation is resistance, and you don't have a resistance problem. You have a follow-through problem in a few specific places, and follow-through problems are fixable.

Here's what the discovery surfaced, in your own words: "Everything still runs through me," "the field finds out about changes when the client mentions them," and "I don't trust the job costing numbers until Susan closes the month." Those three statements map exactly to the three things this engagement exists to fix: your time is the company's bottleneck on estimates, change orders, and approvals; your field and office teams are each working from their own version of the truth; and your financial information arrives weeks after the decisions it should have informed.

Two foundational issues have to be corrected in parallel with training. Your budget templates exist but aren't applied consistently from job to job, and bills are being entered in QuickBooks first and syncing backward into JobTread, which is why your real-time job costing never matches reality. Neither is a rebuild. Both are workflow corrections with a clear owner and timeline.

The path forward: a 12-week Phase 1 implementation that takes estimating, change orders, scheduling, and client communication off your desk and puts them into a system your team already wants to use.

Training Readiness: PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS

Training can begin across most modules immediately. Two foundational corrections must run in parallel during Weeks 1–2, or financial training will produce misleading numbers and erode the team's trust in the system.

Corrective action: introduce the JobTread-first bill workflow in a scheduled Week 2 session with Susan (bookkeeper) and build the SOP together. Timeline: Week 2. Owner: Megan + Susan.

The Three Owner Outcomes

What this engagement changes for you, specifically

Where are you being pulled in today, and what does this free you from?

  • Every estimate starts and ends on your desk. A locked template plus a trained estimator workflow means Travis can build 80% of an estimate before you ever see it.
  • You personally approve every change verbally, then chase the paperwork later. Signed digital change orders mean approval happens once, in writing, without you in the loop twice.
  • Clients call you for status updates. The customer portal answers those calls before they're made.

Where are your teams talking past each other, and what changes?

  • Field learns about scope changes from the client. Fix: change orders publish to the field schedule and daily log the moment they're signed.
  • Office and field keep separate schedules (JobTread vs. Cole's whiteboard). Fix: one restructured schedule both teams actually work from.
  • Susan and the PM reconcile costs by email at month-end. Fix: bills categorized at entry, visible to both the day they arrive.

Where does information stop moving, and where does it start moving on its own?

  • Selections live in Dana's spreadsheet and die there. Fix: the Selections module pushes approved decisions straight into the budget and schedule.
  • Estimate-to-contract handoff is a re-typing exercise. Fix: proposals generate directly from the approved estimate.
  • Close-out learnings never reach the cost catalog. Fix: an end-of-job review loop updates unit costs so every estimate gets sharper.

What to Strengthen First

5. Sharing the load off the owner
Estimating, change orders, and approvals run through Mark by default. Giving the team the tools and authority to carry more is both the goal of the project and a prerequisite for it.

The Honest Bottom Line

The strengths are real: a willing team, a premium client experience, and an owner who knows the business cold. The question this review answers isn't whether Crestline is a good business or a good future client (it's both). It's whether a full implementation succeeds starting from where the account is today. On the evidence, the smarter move is to correct the foundation and protect the time first, which is exactly how Phase 1 is sequenced. Done well, it turns a risky launch into a confident one.

Phase 1 Scope: The 12-Week Engagement

Six two-week blocks, sequenced by business impact

By Week 12, you will be reviewing margin on a dashboard instead of waiting on month-end. Your team will be estimating from one locked template, approving change orders in writing before work begins, and running the field from a schedule they trust. Your information will move from inbox-and-whiteboard to a single system that hands off automatically, from estimate to contract, from contract to field, from field to billing, from close-out back into sharper estimates. And Dana will be holding it accountable so you don't have to.

Proceed conditions corrected here

  • Success Champion confirmation: Dana Whitfield, role defined and announced
