Executive Brief

Written for ownership. This is the discussion page for our review call

Mark, you have built a real company, and the foundation inside JobTread is stronger than you think. Every active job lives in the system. Your cost catalog is genuinely well organized, your vendor and sub records are complete, and your team did not push back on this review. They leaned into it. That matters, because the most expensive part of any implementation is resistance, and you do not have a resistance problem.

What you have is a follow-through problem in a few specific places, and follow-through problems are fixable. Most of what follows comes down to work that is agreed in conversation and never lands anywhere the rest of the company can see it. That is not a software failure. It is the communication and handoff problem you described to us.

Two foundation fixes have to run alongside training. Your budget templates exist but are not applied consistently from job to job, and bills are entered in QuickBooks Online first and synced back into JobTread, which is why your real-time job costing never matches reality. Neither is a rebuild. Both are workflow corrections with a clear owner and a clear week.

How the Fixes Fit Around Training

Nothing here asks the team to stop working while it is repaired

Fix the foundation while the team learns

Both fixes are small in effort and large in effect. The template fix is a decision plus a build, and the bill fix is one change made with Susan followed by a written procedure. Neither needs a migration, a new tool or a pause in how jobs run today.

Order matters. Estimating and cost training start in Week 1, but the numbers they produce are only trusted once the template is locked and the bill workflow is in place. That is why the fixes are scheduled first and why financial training waits for them.

Nobody is replaced and nothing is rebuilt. JobTread already holds every active job, the QuickBooks Online connection already exists, and the cost catalog is already clean. The work is to point each of them in the right direction.

Handling the Money During the Fix

What changes in how bills move, when, and who carries the extra weight

Reverse the door, not the system

Same two systems, in a different order

This is not a migration and nothing is switched off. JobTread and QuickBooks Online stay exactly where they are. What changes is which one a bill is entered into first, and that change is introduced in Week 2.

How bills move today
Vendor bill → QuickBooks Online → JobTread, uncategorized

Susan enters the bill in the books, and it syncs into JobTread with no cost code against the budget. The job looks cheaper than it is until she and the project manager reconcile by email at month-end.

This keeps running exactly as it does now until the replacement exists. Nothing is paused in advance.

How bills move after Week 2
Vendor bill → JobTread, coded → QuickBooks Online

The bill is entered in JobTread and coded to the budget on the day it arrives, then carried into the books. Job cost is right the day the bill is entered rather than the day the month is closed.

The procedure is written together with Susan, so it reflects how her month actually runs.

Any change to the connection is Susan's or a marketplace partner's, not a LuxeCraft job. We do not ask for access to your accounting system or your books. If the way the two systems connect needs to change, that is done by Susan or by a JobTread marketplace partner, and we can point you to one when the timing is right.
⚠ Susan will carry both ways of working for a short time. Bills already in flight finish the old way while new ones start the new way. This is planned and temporary, and it is worth saying out loud now rather than discovering it in week three. She is an outside bookkeeper, so the change is timed around her month-end close.
The rule that keeps the extra work bounded. The same bill is never entered twice. Once the change is made, every bill goes in JobTread first, and the books receive it from there.

The Honest Bottom Line

The strengths are real: a willing team, a premium client experience, and an owner who knows the business cold. Implementation Roadmap

Thirteen weeks, written as the steps that actually get done

Both of you stay heavily involved

  • Dana is the person who will live in the software day to day, and she is already leaning in.
  • Mark, your knowledge is what makes the tools fit the way this company really works, even while you are stepping back from the daily work.
  • Tools built without the experienced owner present are the ones nobody uses six months later.
  • A protected weekly block for each of you is workable, and it needs to be protected rather than hoped for.

Implementation is not the same as institutionalization

  • The build, the process mapping and the training are done for you.
  • It only sticks if you are inside the software between the calls, not just during them.
  • Each week ends with action items on the recap, and those are the homework.
  • Dana co-leads every team rollout, so the knowledge stays in the building.

A note on pace

  • The first few weeks feel slow, because foundations are unglamorous and you cannot see them working yet.
  • Around week four you are using real pieces of the system on real jobs, and the training starts to land.
  • From there it accelerates, because each week builds on something that already works.
  • Cole's field rollout comes after the schedule is right, so the crew meets a tool that helps them.

Please note

This roadmap is a starting plan, not a contract with the calendar. It will change week to week as we learn what your jobs actually need, and anything that moves will be communicated clearly rather than quietly. The order is deliberate: operational clarity first, then the tools that carry it. Target dates are set when we schedule your start.
